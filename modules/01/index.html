Module 01 · Overview

Introduction to Generative AI: What's Really Happening Behind the Chat Box

You've used ChatGPT or Copilot. It feels like talking to someone who has read everything. But as a developer you can't build on a feeling. This module opens the box: what the model actually does with your words, why it sometimes confidently makes things up, and how to choose the right model, and you'll prove each idea with your own hands.

9 topics13 drillsMilestone 1≈ 4 hours hands-on

The mental map

On the first morning of every batch someone asks me: "Is it actually thinking?" My honest answer is that it's doing something much simpler, and much stranger: it predicts the next small piece of text, again and again, very fast. Every page in this module zooms into one part of this picture.

Takeaway

An LLM doesn't look things up and doesn't "know" in the human sense. It produces the most plausible continuation of the text it was given. Almost every GenAI technique you'll learn (prompting, RAG, tools, evaluation) is a way of making "plausible" also mean "correct and useful".

Why developers need to understand what's inside

A team wired an LLM into their support portal in one afternoon. The demo was magical. Two weeks after launch:

"The bot told a learner our refund window is 30 days. It's 14. Legal wants it switched off tonight."A very real Friday-evening escalation

Nothing was broken. The code worked exactly as written. The model did what models do: it produced a plausible answer, because 30 days is a common refund window on the internet. Nobody on the team understood that, so nobody designed for it.

Traditional developer view

Software is deterministic. Same input, same output. A wrong output means a bug to find.

GenAI developer view

The model is probabilistic. Same input can give different outputs, and a wrong answer can come from perfectly working code. You design around the model: give it facts, constrain its output, check its work.

Imagine this

Hiring an LLM is like hiring a brilliant new joiner who has read every book in the library but has never worked at your company, forgets every conversation, and is a little too eager to please. Brilliant at language, bad at saying "I don't know". You wouldn't let them answer customers on their first day without a policy binder and a supervisor. This course is about building that binder and that supervisor.

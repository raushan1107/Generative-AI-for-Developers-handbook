Common GenAI Use Cases for Developers

Most GenAI features are built from a handful of patterns. Learn to see the pattern behind a feature request and you know the architecture, the risks and which module of this course you need. Then test your eye against a model's.

Learn

Many products, a few patterns.

Imagine this

The patterns are kitchen techniques: boil, fry, grill, bake. Thousands of dishes, a handful of techniques. "Support bot", "HR helpdesk" and "legal Q&A" look different on the menu, but in the kitchen they're the same dish: ground the model in documents, let it call a few tools.

In the real world
  • Coding assistants (GitHub Copilot, Claude Code, Codex): generate and transform code, with your files as context, and increasingly act by running commands and tests.
  • Office copilots: summarise meetings and email threads (transform), draft documents (generate), answer from your files (ground).
  • Support assistants in help-desk products: answer from knowledge articles (ground), look up orders or accounts (act), hand over to a human with a summary (transform).
  • Document AI in finance and insurance: extract fields from invoices, claims and contracts into structured data (extract), often from scans (multimodal).
Traditional developer view

"The business wants 'an AI chatbot'. Let's pick a framework."

GenAI developer view

"Which patterns are inside that request? Generate, transform, extract, search, ground, act. Each brings its own architecture, risks and tests."

Takeaway

Translate every GenAI idea into patterns: generate, transform, extract, search, ground, act, and spot the parts that are not GenAI at all.

2

Do

Map feature requests to patterns, then compare with a model.

Drill 01.13

Pattern spotting: map eight feature requests

~15 minDesignAzure OpenAI

Goal: recognise patterns quickly, including the ones that shouldn't use an LLM at all.

  1. Think

    Your answers first

    For each TrainCrest request pick one: generate, transform, extract, search, ground, act, not-genai.

    1. Write a first draft of a course description from the trainer's bullet notes.
    2. Turn a 40-message support thread into a 3-line summary for the next agent.
    3. Pull learner name, enrolment ID and course from incoming emails into the CRM.
    4. Let learners find courses by describing a goal: "I want to automate Excel reports".
    5. Answer "Can I get a refund?" from the current policy document, with a citation.
    6. When a learner asks, look up their real exam voucher status and resend it.
    7. Calculate the refund amount from the cancellation date and course price.
    8. Translate course announcements into Spanish and Japanese.
    Compare with my answers
    1. Generate
    2. Transform
    3. Extract
    4. Search (embeddings)
    5. Ground (RAG)
    6. Act (tool calling)
    7. Not GenAI. Exact arithmetic and business rules belong in code. (The model can call that code: Module 04.)
    8. Transform
  2. Run

    Ask the model to classify the same requests

    """Drill 01.13: let the model sort feature requests into GenAI patterns, then compare with your answers."""
from llm import get_client, MODEL

PATTERNS = ["generate", "transform", "extract", "search", "ground", "act", "not-genai"]

REQUESTS = [
    "Write a first draft of the course description from the trainer's bullet notes.",
    "Turn a 40-message support thread into a 3-line summary for the next agent.",
    "Pull the learner name, enrolment ID and course from incoming emails into our CRM.",
    "Let learners find courses by describing their goal, e.g. 'I want to automate Excel reports'.",
    "Answer 'Can I get a refund?' using the current policy document, with a citation.",
    "When a learner asks, look up their real exam voucher status and resend it.",
    "Calculate the refund amount from the cancellation date and course price.",
    "Translate course announcements into Spanish and Japanese.",
]

client = get_client()
system = (f"Classify each feature request into exactly one pattern from: {', '.join(PATTERNS)}. "
          "'ground' means answering from specific documents; 'act' means calling a system or tool; "
          "'not-genai' means plain code does it better. Reply with one line per request: number, pattern, "
          "and a reason of at most 12 words.")
numbered = "\n".join(f"{i}. {r}" for i, r in enumerate(REQUESTS, 1))
r = client.chat.completions.create(model=MODEL, messages=[
    {"role": "system", "content": system},
    {"role": "user", "content": numbered},
])
print(r.choices[0].message.content.strip())
    $ python lab01_13_patterns.py
  3. Verify
    • Where did you, the model and my answers disagree? Usually on 5 (search vs ground) and 7 (not-genai).
    • Notice the model needed definitions of "ground", "act" and "not-genai" in the system prompt to answer consistently. Unclear categories confuse models just as they confuse people (Module 03).
3

Check

One more.

1. "Build an AI assistant for our HR policies that can also book leave." Which patterns are inside?

Ground (answer from HR policy documents), act (call the leave-booking system, ideally with confirmation), plus generate for the conversation itself.

Learn

The costume, not the script.

Definition

Role prompting assigns the model a persona or perspective (for example "You are a senior support agent at TrainCrest") to shape its tone, vocabulary, level of detail and focus for the task.

Imagine this

Give an actor a doctor's costume and they'll speak like a doctor, walk like a doctor and use the right words. You still wouldn't let them operate. The role is the costume. The knowledge has to come from the script you hand over: the context.

Roles help with

  • Tone and register (warm, formal, playful)
  • Audience level (learner vs engineer)
  • Perspective (reviewer, critic, tester)
  • A consistent persona across a conversation

Roles don't help with

  • Facts the model doesn't have
  • Accuracy on hard reasoning ("world-class mathematician" adds little)
  • A format you never specified
  • Security: a role is not a boundary
In the real world

Product assistants nearly always have a short persona ("You are Contoso's helpful billing assistant…"), but the bulk of their system prompt is rules, context and format. Roles are also used deliberately for perspective: teams ask one model to act as a strict code reviewer or a sceptical evaluator of another model's answer, which you'll see again in evaluation (Module 09).

Common trap

Three paragraphs of flattering persona ("the world's greatest, most experienced, award-winning…") and one line of actual task. Spend those words on context, constraints and examples instead.

Interview cue

"Role prompting shapes style and perspective, not knowledge. I use a short role in the system message and put the facts in the context."

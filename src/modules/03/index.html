The mental map: the prompt design framework

Every good prompt I've seen in production, whatever the model and task, answers the same five questions. The whole module hangs off them.

Takeaway

A prompt isn't a question you ask the model. It's a brief you write for it. The better the brief, the less the model has to guess, and guessing is where unreliability comes from.

Why prompts are specifications

In Module 01 you met the brilliant new joiner who has read everything but knows nothing about your company. The prompt is the only briefing that joiner ever gets. Every time. Nothing you meant reaches the model; only what you wrote.

Imagine this

You walk into a tailor's shop and say "make me a shirt". You'll get a shirt: maybe the wrong size, a collar you hate, winter fabric when you wanted summer, ready next month when the wedding is on Saturday. Now walk in with your measurements, the fabric, "button-down collar, short sleeves, ready by Friday, here's a photo". Same tailor, same skill, completely different result. The brief got better.

Traditional developer view

"The prompt is a string. I'll tweak words until the demo looks good."

GenAI developer view

"The prompt is code running on a probabilistic machine. It has a spec, versions, tests and a review. I change it based on measured results across many inputs, not one lucky example."

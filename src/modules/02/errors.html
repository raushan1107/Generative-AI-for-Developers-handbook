Fix it, or try again later?

Definition

LLM API errors fall into client errors (4xx: bad request, authentication, permission, not found, content policy) that won't succeed if repeated unchanged, and transient errors (429 rate limits, 5xx server errors, timeouts, connection failures) that often succeed on retry. A successful response can also signal a soft failure through finish_reason.

Imagine this

Calling a helpline. "Wrong department" (404) or "you don't have the right form" (400): calling again won't help; you need to change something. "All our lines are busy, please call later" (429, 503): call again, but not every two seconds, or you'll keep the lines busy for everyone.

In the real world
In finance

Error messages in banking must never leak internal details (endpoint names, account numbers, stack traces), and they must never leave a customer guessing about their money. "Something went wrong" after a customer asked "did my transfer go through?" is a bad outcome; a good assistant says it couldn't check, that no action was taken, and how to reach a person. Every error is also logged with a correlation ID, so operations can tie a customer complaint to the exact failed call.

Mature AI products never show raw errors. They map failures to a calm message ("I'm having trouble right now, here's how to reach a human"), log the details with a request ID, alert on configuration errors, and fall back to a second model or region when the primary one is down. Status pages of the big providers show that outages and degradations do happen, so this isn't theoretical.

Common trap

One except Exception that retries everything. A wrong key gets retried forever; a too-long prompt gets retried until the user gives up. Retry only transient errors, and let configuration errors fail loudly in your logs.

Interview cue

"I split errors into retryable (429, 5xx, timeouts, connection) and non-retryable (400, 401, 403, 404), and treat finish_reason length or content_filter as failures even with HTTP 200."

3
Check
Errors.



  
1. Which should be retried automatically: 400, 401, 404, 429, 503, a timeout?
429, 503 and the timeout, with backoff. Not 400, 401 or 404: they need a change to the request or configuration.

  
2. Why must user-facing messages never include the raw error?
Raw errors leak internals (resource and deployment names, sometimes request details) and scare users. Log the details; show a calm message.




Takeaway
Retry what's transient, fix what's configuration, and keep every message human.

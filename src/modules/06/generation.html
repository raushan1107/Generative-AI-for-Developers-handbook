1

Learn

Answer from the sources, all of them, only them.

Definition

Response generation is the final RAG step: the chat model reads the constructed prompt and produces an answer that should be grounded (every claim supported by a source), complete (it uses every relevant source), attributed (it cites them) and honest (it says when the sources don't answer the question), in the user's language and tone.

In plain words

The model writes the answer from the extracts, combines them if the question needs two, points to them, and admits when they don't cover it.

Imagine this

An interpreter at a meeting. A good one renders exactly what was said, in the listener's language, and says "the speaker didn't mention a date" rather than inventing one. A bad one "helps" by filling gaps. You want the first, and you check the transcript afterwards.

Behaviours to design for

Model choice matters more here than anywhere else in RAG. Following "only from the sources, cite each fact, else NOT_FOUND" is an instruction-following skill: larger hosted models do it reliably; small local models often skip citations, ignore the sentinel or add details. Use small models to learn and prototype; measure before shipping (Module 09).

In the real world
In finance

Generation is where a grounded answer can still go wrong: the model may combine two sections into a rule that exists in neither, or round a number. Banks therefore run checks after generation: every number must appear in a cited source, every claim needs a citation, and answers about money that fail a check are replaced with a safe fallback or sent to a person.

Support teams typically roll out RAG answers in stages: first as suggested replies that an agent edits and sends, then as automatic answers for the topics where measured accuracy is high, with a visible "Was this helpful?" and a hand-off to a person. The NOT_FOUND path isn't a failure in that setup; it's the router that sends the hard questions to people.

Traditional developer view

"The model generates the answer; if it's wrong, the model is bad."

GenAI developer view

"Generation is one controlled step with defined exits: no sources, not found, answer. I choose the model by how well it follows grounding rules, and I check its output before anyone reads it."

Common trap

Treating "the answer sounds right" as "the answer is grounded". In this drill, a model answers a refund question in fluent English with a real citation, and still adds a "1-2 billing cycles" claim that no source contains. Only the check in topic 9 notices.

Interview cue

"Generation has explicit exits: skip the model when nothing is retrieved, detect a NOT_FOUND sentinel, otherwise answer at low temperature in the user's language and verify citations. I pick the generator model by its groundedness and citation rate on my evaluation set."

3
Check
Generation.



  
1. Why skip the model call entirely when retrieval returns nothing?
There is nothing to ground an answer in, so any answer would come from the model's memory. Skipping saves cost and latency and guarantees the "not found" path is taken.

  
2. Two retrieved sections give different prices for the same thing. What should the system do?
Prefer the authoritative or newest source by metadata (for example the help centre over an old FAQ, or the most recent updated date) and tell the model that rule; or have it say the sources differ and cite both. Then fix the content, because the conflict will come back.

  
3. When would you use temperature 0 for RAG, and when not?
For factual answers from sources, low temperature gives consistent, literal answers. Some reasoning models don't accept a temperature parameter at all (rag.py retries without it). For creative rewriting of the same facts, a little more is fine.




Takeaway
Generation needs defined exits (no sources, NOT_FOUND, answer), a model that follows grounding rules, and checks on everything it says.

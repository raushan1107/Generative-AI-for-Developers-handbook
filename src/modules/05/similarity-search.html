Learn

Ask in your words, find in theirs.

Definition

Similarity search (semantic search, vector search, k-nearest-neighbour search) retrieves the k stored items whose embeddings are most similar to the query's embedding, returning each item's text, metadata and similarity score.

In plain words

Turn the question into a vector, then fetch the k closest documents. "Cheap intro to containers" lands next to "Docker Quick Start: build, run and share container images", even though the words barely overlap.

Imagine this

A knowledgeable shop assistant. You say "something to keep my coffee hot on the commute" and they hand you three insulated travel mugs, though you never said "insulated", "travel" or "mug". They match what you mean to what they have. They also never say "we have nothing"; ask for a lawnmower in a coffee shop and you'll still be shown the closest thing, which is your cue to add a threshold.

Decisions you make

  • k (how many): a search page shows 5 to 10. RAG (Module 06) usually sends 3 to 8 chunks to the model: too few misses the answer, too many adds cost and noise.
  • Threshold: vector search always returns k results, even for nonsense. Decide what "no good match" looks like for your model (calibrated on real queries), or combine with keywords (topic 8).
  • What to embed: for courses, title plus description; for articles, chunks with their headings (topic 6). Whatever you embed is what can be found.
  • Query side: questions are short and documents long; that asymmetry is normal. Some models want a query prefix (topic 3).
In the real world

Shops, streaming services and job sites all run semantic search behind their search boxes now, usually blended with keyword search and business rules (in stock, in your region, popular). The "no results" page has become rare, which is good for users and dangerous for chatbots: a weak match passed to a model becomes a confident wrong answer.

Traditional developer view

"No matching rows means no results."

GenAI developer view

"Vector search always returns its nearest k. Relevance is a score I have to interpret, so I set k deliberately, watch the scores, and handle 'nothing good' explicitly."

Common trap

Passing the top results to a model without checking relevance. Ask TrainCrest's help sections about "Docker" and vector search returns Ways to pay as the best match (Drill 05.9 shows it). A chatbot would then answer a Docker question with payment details.

Interview cue

"Similarity search embeds the query with the same model as the corpus and returns the top-k nearest items with scores. I tune k for the use case and handle low-relevance results, because vector search never returns an empty set."

Check
Similarity search.



  
1. A learner asks Traincrest Assist about something TrainCrest doesn't offer. What does vector search return?
Still the k nearest items, just with lower scores. The application must notice low relevance (threshold, keyword check, or the model being told to say "not covered") rather than present them as answers.

  
2. Why embed a course's title and description together, rather than only the title?
Titles are short and use internal names; descriptions contain the words and ideas learners use. What you embed is what can be found.




Takeaway
Embed the query with the same model, take the top k, and remember vector search never says "nothing found": that part is your job.

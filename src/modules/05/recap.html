Semantic search checklist

  • Documents and queries use the same embedding model, recorded with the index (Drill 05.1).
  • Vectors are normalised; similarity is cosine; thresholds are calibrated per model (Drill 05.2).
  • The embedding model was chosen on your own labelled questions and languages (Drill 05.3).
  • Every vector is stored with its text, source and filterable metadata (Drills 05.4, 05.5).
  • Searches handle "nothing relevant" instead of trusting the top k blindly (Drill 05.6).
  • Chunks follow the document's structure, carry their titles, and were chosen by hit rate (Drill 05.7).
  • Hard rules and permissions are pre-filters built from typed values (Drill 05.8).
  • Keyword and vector results are merged by rank, with a proper analyzer, and measured (Drill 05.9).

Self-check

1. In one sentence, what is an embedding and why does it enable semantic search?

A vector representation of meaning, where similar texts land close together, so "find similar" becomes "find the nearest vectors", whatever words were used.

2. Your similarity scores all sit between 0.3 and 0.7. Is something broken?

Probably not: score ranges are model-specific. Look at rankings, and calibrate any threshold on labelled examples for that model.

3. You switch the embed deployment to a new model. What breaks, and what do you do?

Old vectors and new query vectors are in different spaces (maybe different sizes), so results become meaningless. Re-embed everything, rebuild the index, and compare hit rates before switching traffic.

4. Fixed 800-character chunks scored best in Drill 05.7. Why did we still choose heading chunks?

The bank's articles are short, so 800-character chunks are half an article and nearly always contain the answer. But they send about three times the text to the model in RAG and can't be cited precisely. Heading chunks found every answer in the top 3, are one idea each, and cite as "Fees and charges › Overdrafts".

5. "Cards with no annual fee for earning rewards abroad" returns nothing with post-filtering. Explain and fix.

The top k by meaning were a card with a $95 fee and two savings accounts, and filtering afterwards removed them all. Use pre-filtering, so ranking happens only among products that satisfy the filter.

6. What does Reciprocal Rank Fusion combine, and why not just add scores?

It combines ranks from the keyword and vector lists (Σ 1/(60 + rank)). BM25 and cosine scores are on different scales, so adding them lets one side dominate.

Learn

How you cut decides what you find.

Definition

Chunking splits documents into smaller passages that are embedded and indexed separately. Common strategies are fixed-size (a number of characters or tokens, often with overlap), sentence or paragraph based, and structure-based (by headings, sections, pages or code functions). Each chunk keeps a link to its source document.

In plain words

One vector per whole article means one blurry summary. One vector per section means a sharp vector for "how much do I get back?" and another for "when does the money arrive?", so a question can land on exactly the right one.

Imagine this

Index cards for a cookbook. One card per book ("Italian food") won't help you find the risotto. One card per word is useless too. One card per recipe, with the book title on top, is just right: specific enough to find, complete enough to cook from.

The trade-offs

ChoiceSmallerLarger
Chunk sizePrecise matches, but may lose context ("it costs $49" — what does?)More context, but the vector blurs and the answer is diluted
OverlapLess duplication, but a rule split at a boundary can be lostSafer boundaries, more chunks to store and embed
Context headerShorter chunks"Labs › How long labs stay open" makes the chunk understandable alone
  • Structure first: markdown headings, HTML sections, PDF pages, code functions, FAQ question-answer pairs. Fixed-size windows are for walls of text.
  • Add context to each chunk: the document and section title (and maybe a date or product) at the top. It helps both retrieval and the model that reads it later.
  • Keep the source: store source (file, URL, page) with every chunk. Module 06 uses it for citations.
  • Re-chunking means re-embedding: treat the chunking strategy like the embedding model: versioned, measured, rebuilt deliberately.
In the real world

Teams building "chat with our documentation" bots typically spend more time on chunking and cleaning than on prompts: stripping navigation menus from web pages, keeping tables intact, splitting PDFs by section rather than by page, and adding titles to every chunk. Azure AI Search can chunk for you with built-in skills during indexing, but you still choose the strategy and measure it.

Traditional developer view

"Split the text every 1,000 characters and index it."

GenAI developer view

"Chunks are the units of retrieval. I cut along the document's meaning, give each chunk its context, keep its source, and pick size and overlap by measuring hit rate on real questions."

Common trap

Judging chunking by eye ("these chunks look reasonable") instead of by retrieval. Drill 05.7's fixed 800-character chunks look tidy, put the right chunk in the top 3 every time, and still put it first only 15 times out of 20.

Interview cue

"I chunk by the document's structure where possible, add document and section titles to each chunk, use fixed-size windows with overlap as a fallback, store the source for citations, and choose size and overlap by measuring retrieval hit rate on labelled questions."

3
Check
Chunking.



  
1. A chunk reads "It costs $49 for 14 more days." Why is that a retrieval problem, and how do you fix it?
Out of context, nothing says what costs $49, so questions about lab extensions may not match it. Add the context header ("Labs and cloud sandboxes › How long labs stay open") or chunk by section so the subject travels with the fact.

  
2. Why use overlap with fixed-size chunking?
So a sentence or rule that straddles a boundary appears whole in at least one chunk. The cost is some duplication in storage and embeddings.

  
3. You change from 300- to 500-character chunks. What else must happen?
Re-chunk and re-embed the whole corpus, rebuild the index, and re-measure the hit rate to confirm the change helped.




Takeaway
Cut along the document's structure, give every chunk its context and source, and choose the strategy by hit rate, not by eye.
